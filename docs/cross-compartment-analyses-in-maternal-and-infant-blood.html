Section 10 Cross-compartment analyses in maternal and infant blood

This chapter reports the cross-compartment analyses conducted in the MISAME-III subset with paired maternal plasma, maternal and infant dried-blood microsample (VAMS) metabolomics, and maternal blood proteomics, alongside the milk measurements analysed elsewhere in this appendix. It supports the manuscript Results subsection “Intervention effects are mirrored in maternal and infant circulation” and corresponds to manuscript Fig. S7 and Table S8.

Scope. The milk analyses in the rest of this appendix (§1–§9) pool all three trials (MISAME-III, Mumta-LW, ELICIT). The blood and cross-compartment work in this chapter is MISAME-III only — maternal plasma, dried-blood VAMS metabolomics, and blood proteomics were collected only in MISAME-III, so there is no Mumta-LW or ELICIT blood. MISAME-III used a 2×2 randomisation of prenatal × postnatal balanced energy–protein (BEP) supplementation, giving four arms (Control = IFA/IFA, prenatal-only = BEP/IFA, postnatal-only = IFA/BEP, both periods = BEP/BEP); the postnatal exposure is the lactation-period contrast of interest for milk-borne transfer.

Visit / timepoint legend. Blood tables use MISAME-III visit codes; milk tables use calendar labels. tri3 = third trimester · incl = enrolment · acco = birth/accouchement · pn12 = 1–2 months postpartum · pn34 = 3–4 months · pn56 = 5–6 months. In prose we use plain “months”; the codes appear only in the data tables.

Two framing constraints apply to every result below:

  • Direction, not magnitude, is comparable across platforms. Absolute feature intensities from milk, plasma, and the two VAMS matrices come from different instruments and normalisations, so they do not sit on a common scale. Every cross-compartment comparison therefore concerns only the sign (up/down) of the within-dataset intervention effect, never the size of an intensity.
  • These analyses are exploratory. The adjusted TMLE models are the primary basis here (unadjusted models are retained upstream as sensitivity), and where both an adjusted and unadjusted table exist the *_adjusted input is shown. Feature identities from the untargeted platforms are putative (mass-based, MSI level ≈ 3) unless otherwise stated.

The single most reused number below: where maternal and infant blood shared one feature catalogue at 5–6 months, BEP-responsive features agreed in direction for 16 of 18 maternally-significant features; a medium-chain acylcarnitine (m/z 286.202, provisionally octenoylcarnitine) rose in milk, maternal plasma, and infant blood in postnatally-supplemented arms only, with doubly-robust mediation attributing ≈30% of the infant effect to milk.

10.2 Cross-compartment concordance of BEP-responsive features

Do the features that respond to BEP move in the same direction in different compartments? For each directed comparison (“arrow”, e.g. milk → maternal plasma) and contrast, we matched features across datasets and quantified concordance at a nominal threshold and threshold-free (rank–rank hypergeometric overlap, weighted correlation, directional GSEA, and an anchored subset restricted to jointly-significant features).

How features are matched (ppm-only). Cross-platform matching uses accurate mass and ionisation mode only, applying Kim’s experimental-over-experimental tolerance

\[\text{ppm} = \frac{|m_1 - m_2|}{m}\times 10^{6} \le 25,\]

where both masses are measured (there is no theoretical/reference mass in the denominator). Retention time was made optional and is not used in the first-pass match. This deliberately departs from Kim’s advice to keep RT, because the platforms run different chromatography; RT re-enters only as an annotation-support check for the carnitine feature (§10.4). Two consequences follow, and both are load-bearing for interpretation:

  • ±25 ppm is a hypothesis-generation screen, not proof of same-molecule identity. A 25-ppm, RT-free match on untargeted data can pair isobaric but chemically distinct features. Every cross-platform, mass-only match here is therefore putative and pending Kim-level annotation.
  • Identity-anchored links are far stronger than mass-only links. Where maternal and infant postnatal VAMS share one feature catalogue, matches are by shared identifier (like-for-like), not by mass.

Matching-confidence ladder (how much to trust each link). Three kinds of link appear in this chapter, in decreasing reliability: (1) shared identifier — maternal ↔︎ infant VAMS, the same catalogue, an exact-feature match; (2) same-method mass — datasets on the same chromatography (e.g. milk / plasma / prenatal VAMS), where a ±25 ppm match is well-behaved; (3) cross-method mass — anything matched to postnatal VAMS across chromatography, where a ±25 ppm mass-only match is identity-uncertain and needs annotation. Read tier-3 rows as leads to confirm, not as established identities.

Interpretation note. Concordance is strongest where feature identity is anchored — maternal and infant VAMS share a single catalogue, so their agreement (16 of 18 maternally-significant features at 5–6 months) reflects like-for-like features. Cross-platform mass-matching is near chance in bulk; only the jointly-FDR-significant subset is essentially fully concordant. Read the bulk mass-matched percentages as a floor, not a biological effect size.

Figure. Left: standardized adjusted effects of matched features, one compartment against another, coloured by joint significance. Right: rank–rank hypergeometric overlap of maternal vs. infant postnatal VAMS at 5–6 months, showing concentrated concordance among co-up and co-down features.

The full per-pair matched-feature tables (cross_compartment_metab_*_adjusted.csv, tens of thousands of rows each) are not shipped with this appendix — they are too large to render or download usefully; the per-arrow summaries above and the paired scatter figures (figures/cross_compartment/metab_*_adjusted.png) capture their content, and the full tables can be regenerated from the upstream blood pipeline.

10.3 Selenoproteins concordantly increased in milk and maternal blood

The milk and maternal blood proteomes were overlapped by UniProt accession. The overlap table holds 302 protein × assay comparisons (215 unique UniProt proteins, each compared against the immunodepleted and/or naive maternal-blood assay). Only two proteins are FDR-significantly increased in both compartments: SELENOP (selenoprotein P, P49908) and GPX3 (glutathione peroxidase 3, P22352), both of them selenium-transport or selenium-dependent antioxidant proteins. This is the protein-level counterpart to the milk selenium and glutathione-peroxidase signals.

10.4 Octenoylcarnitine milk → infant transfer and mediation

The clearest transfer signal is a medium-chain acylcarnitine feature at m/z 286.202 (provisionally octenoylcarnitine). It is intrinsically an arm-stratified result: the effect is confined to the arms that received BEP after birth and is absent in the prenatal-only arm.

Arm-stratified infant blood at 1–2 months (pn12), the contemporaneous lead. Against Control (IFA/IFA), the m/z 286.202 feature is FDR-significantly increased in the two postnatally-supplemented arms and not in the prenatal-only arm:

  • IFA/BEP (postnatal-only): +1.27 SD (95% CI 1.00, 1.55; two co-eluting split features at 286.202, +1.26 and +1.27) — the largest effect;
  • BEP/BEP (both periods): +0.90 SD (95% CI 0.64, 1.17);
  • BEP/IFA (prenatal-only): not FDR-significant (no 286.202 signal).

(Per-arm analytic n at infant pn12: Control 78, IFA/BEP 86, BEP/IFA 57, BEP/BEP 64, all estimable.) A signal that is largest in the postnatal-only arm and null in the prenatal-only arm is the pattern expected of a postnatal, milk-borne transfer rather than prenatal carryover. Under the pooled postnatal-BEP contrast the same feature is increased in every measured compartment (milk +0.95, maternal plasma +1.33, postnatal maternal VAMS +0.78, infant VAMS +1.04 SD); the pn34/pn56 visits are consistent but subordinate to this contemporaneous pn12 result.

Mediation. Each mother’s milk level of the feature predicted her own infant’s blood level, and doubly-robust (medoutcon TMLE) natural-effects mediation attributed ≈30% of the BEP → infant effect to the milk path (natural indirect effect NIE = 0.29, 95% CI 0.11–0.48; proportion mediated 0.32; the Baron–Kenny dyadic estimator agrees at 0.30). Because the milk mediator is measured with error, this ~30% is a lower bound (the measurement-error sensitivity table shows the corrected proportion rising as assumed reliability falls).

Identity caveat. The m/z 286.202 assignment is provisional and mass-based. Three in-repo checks (accurate mass, co-feature search, homolog elution order — mass/RT only, no MS/MS) favour octenoylcarnitine over the near-twin octanoylcarnitine (the feature sits within ~2.5 ppm of octenoylcarnitine [M+H]⁺ on the VAMS catalogue, and octanoylcarnitine resolves as a separate feature). But the targeted Biocrates panel does not corroborate a named acylcarnitine (octanoylcarnitine is null; the acylcarnitine composition is unshifted, all changes < 6%), and the milk-side feature sits ~11 ppm off the milk homolog ladder, so the milk → infant identity link awaits Kim-level MS/MS annotation. The transfer/mediation conclusion rests on the untargeted feature and its dyadic behaviour, not on a confirmed structure.

10.5 Pathway-level signals across compartments

Exploratory — read as hypothesis, not confirmation. Mummichog pathway enrichment is direction-agnostic and unstable on the short feature lists here (3–18 features per stratum); it is putative-annotation / hypothesis-generation only. The load-bearing cross-compartment results are the identity-anchored concordance (§10.2), the selenoprotein chain (§10.3), and the octenoylcarnitine transfer + mediation (§10.4) above — not the enrichment heatmap below.

We ran Mummichog functional enrichment (direction-agnostic) and complementary signed sign-tests on the blood and milk feature lists. The picture that emerges is a fat-synthesis redistribution: de novo fatty-acid biosynthesis and related lipid pathways are down in milk but up in maternal blood, consistent with mothers making less milk fat themselves and drawing more on circulating or dietary fat. The signed sign-tests confirm this direction, which the direction-agnostic Mummichog enrichment cannot, and it holds across the three milk timepoints.

10.6 Putative annotation of FDR-significant features

Because the metabolomic platforms are untargeted, the FDR-significant features carry only putative annotations, assigned by accurate mass against reference libraries (MSI level ≈ 3), with most features returning several candidates or none. The tables below list the mass-based candidate annotations for the blood and milk FDR-significant features; the curated milk list includes ~48 named metabolites (e.g. pantothenate/B5, ascorbate, fumarate). 10.7 Is the transferred signal from the BEP product itself?

A provenance check asked whether the intervention-up features are simply high-abundance components of the BEP product passing directly into milk or blood. They are not. Screening each compartment’s up-regulated features against the supplement composition, only 1 of 15 BEP-up maternal features and 0 of 11 BEP-up infant features are high-abundance product components, neither exceeding the ~3% background rate expected by chance. The leading transferred feature, the m/z 286.202 acylcarnitine, is absent from the product (the nearest product feature in that mass region is ~35 ppm away and itself depleted), and octenoylcarnitine is an endogenous fatty-acid-oxidation intermediate rather than a listed ingredient. The most likely reading is therefore a treatment-driven endogenous metabolic response transmitted through milk, not direct dietary passage of a supplement ingredient: the product plausibly drives the response by supplying substrate (fatty acids and amino-acid precursors) rather than the metabolite itself.

One honest caveat. A weak direct-passage component is not fully excluded: supplement-abundant features are modestly over-represented among BEP-up maternal plasma features (Fisher p ≈ 3×10⁻⁴ in the tracer table below, vs p ≈ 0.22 in milk), and one maternal feature is strongly supplement-abundant (a direct-transfer candidate worth annotating). The acylcarnitine-specific absence is solid; generalising “endogenous, not dietary” to the entire increased signal awaits Kim-level annotation of these cross-platform matches.

10.8 Dyadic whole-profile similarity and growth (negative controls)

Two negative-control checks bound the interpretation. First, does BEP raise the whole-profile metabolomic similarity between a mother and her own infant, beyond the specific transferred features? It does not; dyadic distance and correlation do not differ by arm. Second, is the transferred infant carnitine signal associated with 6-month infant growth? It is not. So a specific, mediated transfer of individual features can coexist with no global dyadic convergence and no downstream growth effect: transmission ≠ growth effect.
