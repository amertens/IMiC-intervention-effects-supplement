Section 9 Reproducible pathway and enrichment analyses

This chapter reports the pathway- and enrichment-level interpretation of the per-component milk signals shown in §4 and §5, converting large tables of individual features into a smaller set of biologically interpretable pathways. It replaces the earlier manual metaboanalyst.ca point-and-click workflow with a fully scripted MetaboAnalystR pipeline (src/metaboanalyst/ in the upstream analysis repository), so that every table below can be regenerated deterministically from the intervention-effect estimates.

Each analysis is reported twice: for the combined-arm contrast (all maternal nutritional-intervention arms pooled vs. control — the framing used in the printed manuscript figures) and for the arm-stratified contrasts (each randomised arm vs. control — the supplement view). Enrichment tests use a permissive foreground (features at unadjusted p < 0.05, split by direction of effect) evaluated against the assay-specific background, then Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction of the pathway p-values within each study × timepoint × contrast × direction stratum (and, for Mummichog, ionisation mode). A pathway is flagged significant at Q < 0.05 (fdr_native / fdr / FDR column, depending on the table).

Interpretation note. These enrichment results summarise and contextualise the prespecified per-component analyses; they are not themselves a separate confirmatory test. Over-representation depends on the choice of background metabolite set (see §9.2), and pathway membership in reference databases is incomplete, so absence of a pathway is not evidence of absence of biology. Read the results as a structured, hypothesis-generating map of where the intervention signal concentrates.

Coverage — all six manuscript enrichment tables (S1–S6) are reproduced here as searchable tables, in manuscript order: primary ORA (Table S1, §9.1), the ORA-vs-impact comparison (§9.2), the empty secondary result (§9.3), tertiary MSEA (Table S2, §9.4), triglyceride fatty-acid composition (Table S3, §9.5), untargeted-metabolome MSEA (Table S4, §9.6), milk untargeted Mummichog (Table S5, §9.7), and milk-proteome GO (Table S6, §9.8).

9.2 Primary outcomes — MetaboAnalyst enrichment of micronutrient / B-vitamin features

These are the pathway summaries of the primary-outcome signals (macronutrients, micronutrients, B-vitamins). They correspond to manuscript Table S1 and the enrichment panels of manuscript Fig. 3B. Over-representation analysis (ORA) is run per study × timepoint × contrast × direction against the targeted-panel background; the significant column flags pathways with fdr_native < 0.05.

The clearest result is nicotinate and nicotinamide metabolism, driven by the ELICIT nicotinamide-during-lactation arm (up-regulated at both the 1-month and 5-month visits; 5/15 pathway metabolites hit, hypergeometric p ≈ 3.7 × 10⁻¹¹, Q ≈ 3.0 × 10⁻⁹), and also FDR-significant in the BEP trials (Mumta-LW, MISAME-III). Riboflavin, vitamin B6, and thiamine metabolism appear as secondary FDR-significant pathways across BEP trials and timepoints.

9.3 Primary outcomes — over-representation vs. quantitative pathway/impact analysis

MetaboAnalyst supports two related enrichment styles that answer subtly different questions, and comparing them makes the background dependence of enrichment explicit:

  • Over-representation analysis (ORA) — the §9.1 tables — tests whether the significant features are unexpectedly concentrated in a pathway, using the restricted targeted-panel as the background universe.
  • Pathway / impact analysis — the table below — additionally weights each pathway by the topological position of the hit metabolites and evaluates it against the full SMPDB background.

Both recover nicotinate and nicotinamide metabolism as the leading primary-outcome pathway. The impact analysis additionally flags vitamin B6 metabolism; because that difference tracks the larger background set rather than a distinct data signal, we show the two side by side so the dependence stays visible.

9.5 Tertiary outcomes — targeted-metabolome MSEA

Metabolite-set enrichment analysis (MSEA) of the tertiary Biocrates MxP Quant 500 targeted panel summarises the per-metabolite effects from §5 at the pathway level. It corresponds to manuscript Table S2 and Fig. 5B.

Under BEP supplementation, breastmilk metabolism undergoes time-dependent reprogramming. In the early postpartum period the enrichment is dominated by broad down-regulation of amino-acid and nitrogen-handling pathways (arginine–proline, glycine–serine, methionine, and glutamate metabolism, and ammonia recycling) together with mitochondrial energy metabolism (fatty-acid β-oxidation, the citric-acid cycle, the electron-transport chain, ketone-body metabolism, and carnitine synthesis). By 3–4 months several of these pathways reverse, showing enrichment of mitochondrial respiration, lipid oxidation, and ketone metabolism. (The distinct down-regulation of de novo fatty-acid biosynthesis is an untargeted-metabolome finding — see §9.6 — not a targeted-panel MSEA result.)

9.6 Triglyceride fatty-acid composition

The compositional companion to the fat-adjusted triglyceride analysis in §8.3, corresponding to manuscript Table S3 and Fig. 5C. For each study × timepoint × contrast × direction stratum, the fatty-acyl moieties carried by the intervention-responsive triglycerides are compared against the background TG pool (a Wilcoxon test on within-TG proportional representation across the sn-1/sn-2/sn-3 positions). This identifies fatty acids compositionally enriched or depleted among the BEP-responsive triglycerides — not absolute fatty-acid concentrations. The consistent signal is enrichment of polyunsaturated fatty acids — α-linolenic (18:3n-3), γ-linolenic, and related PUFAs — among the triglycerides increased under BEP across MISAME-III contrasts and timepoints. The signal resolves in the arm-stratified analysis (the combined-arm table is empty).

9.7 Untargeted-metabolome MSEA

Metabolite-set enrichment of the untargeted milk-metabolome feature lists, corresponding to manuscript Table S4 and Fig. 6A. This is the named-metabolite-set counterpart to the Mummichog view in §9.7 — both operate on the same untargeted features, but this enrichment tests curated metabolite sets on name-mapped compounds whereas Mummichog infers pathways directly from m/z. The table is read verbatim from the 14 MetaboAnalyst enrichment exports (one per study × timepoint × direction cell) downloaded for the manuscript, rather than regenerated by a script, and reproduces the submitted signature: the recurring pathways are Fatty Acid Biosynthesis, Warburg Effect, Urea Cycle, Citric Acid Cycle, and Mitochondrial Electron Transport Chain, with points from both MISAME-III and Mumta-LW (not MISAME-III alone). Backgrounds are per-study name-mapped reference metabolomes (246 compounds for MISAME-III, 234 for the ELICIT/Mumta-LW–Vital platform). Labels and thresholds are on the raw p-value (grey at P < 0.05, green at P < 0.01); no pathway clears FDR under this reproduction (the smallest fdr_native is ≈ 0.15). The strongest raw-p signals are a co-ordinated up-regulation of Warburg Effect / Citric Acid Cycle / Urea Cycle / mitochondrial ETC in Mumta-LW at 1.5 mo. and a down-regulation of Fatty Acid Biosynthesis in MISAME-III.

Reproducibility note. This enrichment is compound-name-matching- and background-dependent and is not scriptably reproducible from the raw features (it reads MetaboAnalyst web-tool exports, and the mapped-name set and reference background are fixed at export time). The identification-robust untargeted view is the Mummichog analysis in §9.7, which works directly from m/z.

9.8 Exploratory untargeted milk metabolome — Mummichog functional enrichment

Mummichog infers enriched pathways directly from mass-spectral features (without prior compound identification), matching m/z to putative compounds within each ionisation mode. Annotation uses a 10 ppm database tolerance against the human_mfn reference network, with pathways tested at p < 0.05. This is the exploratory untargeted-metabolome enrichment, corresponding to manuscript Table S5 and Fig. 6B, reported by study × timepoint × contrast × direction × ionisation mode.

Two tolerances, one number apart. The 10 ppm here is a compound-annotation tolerance (matching a feature’s m/z to a database compound). It is a different quantity from the ±25 ppm cross-platform feature-matching tolerance used in §10.2 (matching a feature in one dataset to a feature in another). A reviewer comparing the two chapters should not read the difference as an inconsistency.

The dominant signature is down-regulation of polyunsaturated-fatty-acid and lipid pathways — de novo fatty-acid biosynthesis, fatty-acid activation and metabolism, arachidonic-acid and linoleate metabolism, and leukotriene / prostaglandin formation — concentrated in the MISAME-III BEP contrasts. The full grid is large (≈9,500 pathway × stratum rows), so the table below is filtered to the FDR-significant rows.

9.8.1 Feature-level putative annotation

The pathway view above is complemented by the finalized per-feature annotation of the same untargeted milk features. Across 1,034 FDR-significant features, organised by direction × ionisation mode, 243 carry a name (48 curated + 195 Mummichog-putative) and the remainder are unresolved; the strongest named up-regulated signal is a vitamin/cofactor cluster (pantothenate/B5, nicotinamide, ascorbate). Every name is putative — mass-based, MSI ~level 3 — not a confirmed identification.

9.9 Exploratory untargeted milk proteome — GO functional enrichment

Gene Ontology (Biological Process) over-representation of the intervention-associated untargeted milk proteomic features corresponds to manuscript Table S6 and Fig. 6C. Enrichment uses a UniProt-native over-representation test — clusterProfiler::enricher run against a custom UniProt→GO-BP annotation map, with each measured protein counted once and a per-cell measured-proteome background (the set of proteins actually quantified in that study × timepoint × contrast). This supersedes the earlier UniProt→Entrez→enrichGO gene-level workflow, whose identifier-mapping step distorted the background and produced a now-retracted “three-domain” result.

The corrected signature is a predominantly down-regulated shift — no GO-BP term is FDR-significantly up-regulated in the primary BEP contrasts:

  • MISAME-III (1–2 mo., BEP): 31 FDR-significant down-regulated GO-BP terms, dominated by proteostasis, translation, protein folding, and intracellular trafficking. The telomere-maintenance and DNA-templated terms that head the list are driven by molecular chaperones — the CCT/TCP1 chaperonin subunits together with HSP90AA1/AB1 and the co-chaperone PTGES3 — appearing under those GO labels because chaperones annotate to telomere/DNA-repair complexes; this is a chaperone/proteostasis signal, not a telomere-specific effect.
  • Mumta-LW (2 mo., BEP+ExBf): 65 FDR-significant down-regulated terms centred on energy metabolism — glycolysis, pyruvate metabolism, cellular respiration, ATP generation, and nucleotide / NAD (pyridine-nucleotide) metabolism — driven by glycolytic and redox enzymes (PKM, GAPDH, ALDOA/ALDOC, PGAM1, GPI, LDHA, G6PD, IDH2, NAMPT).

Count reconciliation (31/65 vs 27/58). The counts cited above (31 MISAME-III, 65 Mumta-LW) are those reported in the authoritative manuscript Table S6 (Manuscript/Table_S6_proteomics_GO.xlsx). This appendix’s scripted re-run of the same UniProt-native pipeline returns 27 and 58 FDR-significant terms — a ~1% discrepancy near the FDR cutoff attributable to a GO-annotation-version drift between the two runs, not a methodological difference. The table below is the scripted output (27 + 58 = 85 down-regulated FDR-significant rows).

Proteomic-interpretation caveat (GO BP). Milk proteomic features draw from a heterogeneous mixture of cellular debris (mammary epithelial cells, leukocytes, exosomes) and secreted proteins. Enrichment of terms such as “telomere maintenance” or “glycolytic process” therefore indicates a signature consistent with changes in cellular composition or intracellular protein content in milk, not that the named process is being executed within secreting cells. Read these as hypothesis-generating; mechanistic claims require functional assays that were not part of the IMiC protocol.
