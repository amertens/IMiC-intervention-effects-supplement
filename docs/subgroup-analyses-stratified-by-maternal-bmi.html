Section 3 Subgroup analyses stratified by maternal BMI

Did the intervention effects on human milk components depend on maternal nutritional status at baseline? We addressed this using maternal body mass index (BMI) as a marker of that status. BMI was dichotomized a priori into underweight (BMI ≤ 18.5 kg/m²) and normal or overweight (BMI > 18.5 kg/m²) strata. We estimated stratum-specific ATEs with the same TMLE framework used in the primary adjusted analyses. Interaction p-values come from a Wald-type test comparing the two stratum-specific estimates, taking the difference in estimates scaled by the pooled standard error.

The main manuscript cites this section as “Online Appendix 3” in the Results paragraphs on macronutrient and micronutrient effects, where it reports no consistent evidence of effect modification by maternal BMI across primary, secondary, and tertiary outcomes. The plots and tables below let a reader check that conclusion component by component.

Interpretation note. These subgroup analyses are exploratory and not powered to detect modest interactions, particularly within studies where the underweight stratum is small. Where per-component point estimates differ in magnitude or direction across BMI strata, read them cautiously, especially when the corresponding 95% CIs are wide.

3.1 Per-component stratified forest plots

Figure (Online Appendix 3). Adjusted ATEs (SD units, with 95% CI) within maternal-BMI strata (underweight, BMI ≤ 18.5 kg/m²; normal/overweight, BMI > 18.5 kg/m²), by study and milk-collection visit, shown separately for primary, secondary, and tertiary outcomes. Points are coloured by BMI stratum; hover (HTML) for the stratum-specific estimate, q-value, and the Wald-type interaction p-value. As noted above, these analyses are exploratory and not powered to detect modest interactions.
